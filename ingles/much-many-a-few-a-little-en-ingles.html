Much, many, a few, a little: cuantificadores en inglés

Para hablar de cantidades, el inglés distingue entre sustantivos contables (que se pueden contar: books) e incontables (que no: water, money, advice).

Much y many

Many con contables: How many books? Much con incontables: How much money? En frases afirmativas se prefiere a lot of: I have a lot of friends.

A few y a little

A few con contables (a few friends) y a little con incontables (a little time) significan 'algunos/un poco'. Sin la a (few, little) el sentido es negativo: casi ninguno.

Some, any, too y enough

Some en afirmativas y ofrecimientos (Would you like some tea?), any en negativas y preguntas, too much/many = demasiado y enough = suficiente.

Ejemplos con audio

¿Cuántos libros tienes?
How many books do you have?
No tengo mucho dinero.
I don't have much money.
Tenemos unos pocos huevos.
We have a few eggs.
Tomemos un poco de agua.
Let's have a little water.
Hay demasiada gente.
There are too many people.
Me dio un consejo.
She gave me some advice.

Errores comunes

  • I don't have many money. → I don't have much money.
    Money es incontable.
  • She gave me an advice. → She gave me some advice.
    Advice es incontable.
  • There are too much people. → There are too many people.
    People es contable.

Practica ahora

Preguntas frecuentes

¿Money es contable?

No, es incontable: much money, not many money.

¿Qué significa few sin a?

Casi ninguno; con a few significa algunos.
